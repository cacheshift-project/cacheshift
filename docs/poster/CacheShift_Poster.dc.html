What an answer cache really does to an AI app's cost and correctness: measured, and fixed.

The problem

Small teams cut their AI bill with a cache, which reuses a saved answer when a new question looks alike, and a router, which sends easy questions to a cheap model and hard ones to an expensive one. Together they can quietly go wrong: a question gets another question's answer, or the router drifts off its budget.

Every request is logged. The log feeds a one-page report (wrong answers first), a safe-setting finder that replays your traffic at each setting, and a live dashboard.

How we tested it

Recorded GPT-4 and Mixtral answers from RouterBench, scored against official keys on ARC and 13,724 MMLU questions, plus 777k real chatbot prompts. 95% ranges over question groups; each plan committed before its run.

What didn't hold

  • Our first headline, "caching breaks routing budgets": the drift was too small.
  • Who the user is: the planned interviews never happened.

A setting that was safe on ARC caused 567 wrong answers on MMLU (4.1%). Most were questions sharing a long template or passage, such as "For which of these two scenarios…": the cache matched the shared text, not the question.

The fix: compare only what differs

Before reusing an answer, trim the words the two questions share and compare the rest. MMLU: wrong answers 567 → 67 (−88%). Tested unchanged on HellaSwag: only −35%, as different questions about the same video really are alike.

Which setting is safe?

Our finder replays your traffic at every setting and picks the most savings under 1 wrong answer in 200: 0.95 on ARC and HellaSwag, 0.95 + the check on MMLU, still answering half from the cache.

If you run a cache

  • Turn it on: it halves the bill.
  • Count wrong answers on your own questions; use the difference check if they share long context.
  • Re-tune only with a few thousand requests behind it.
